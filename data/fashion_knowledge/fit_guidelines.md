# Clothing Fit Guidelines

This guide describes what fit means, how to recognize it, and how different fit categories behave. Fit is partly technical and partly personal. There is no single universal fit, and comfort, culture, climate and occasion all shape what feels right.

## General Fit Principles

- Fit describes how a garment sits on the body in relation to its natural lines: shoulders, chest, waist, hips and length.
- A well-fitting garment allows comfortable movement and holds its shape without pulling or bunching.
- Fit is judged while standing, sitting, raising the arms and walking.
- Fabric stretch, weight and structure change how the same cut fits.
- Measurements and size charts differ by brand, so trying a garment or checking measurements is more reliable than trusting the size label alone.
- Tailoring can adjust many fit issues, especially hems, sleeve length and waist.

## Fit Categories

### Tight
- Closely follows the body and leaves little ease.
- Can restrict movement and show pulling at seams or buttons.

### Slim
- Follows the body's outline with a small amount of ease.
- Works well for people who like a streamlined look.

### Regular
- Has moderate ease through the chest, waist and hips.
- Allows comfortable movement and is widely adaptable.

### Relaxed
- Has generous ease and falls loosely away from the body.
- Suits warm weather, casual settings and comfort-first dressing.

### Oversized
- Is intentionally cut much larger than the body, often with dropped shoulders and extra length.
- Works best when the proportions are deliberate, often balanced by a fitted piece elsewhere.

## Shoulders

- The shoulder seam of a shirt, jacket or kurta generally sits near the edge of the shoulder.
- A seam that sits well inside the shoulder can feel tight; one that hangs past the shoulder gives a dropped effect.
- Dropped shoulders are intentional in oversized styles but usually undesirable in tailored ones.
- Shoulder fit is difficult to alter, so it is worth checking first.

## Chest / Bust

- Chest or bust areas should close without pulling, gaping or strain on buttons.
- Horizontal wrinkles across the chest can indicate tightness.
- Extra loose fabric at the chest can suggest the garment is larger than needed.
- For women's blouses and kurtis, check comfort when raising arms and sitting.

## Waist

- The waist of a fitted garment should follow the natural waist without squeezing.
- A comfortable waist allows sitting, breathing and eating without discomfort.
- Loose garments can skim past the waist, and belts or ties can add definition when desired.

## Sleeves

- Shirt sleeves generally end near the wrist bone for a standard look, and slightly shorter for relaxed or rolled styles.
- Jacket sleeves typically show a small amount of shirt cuff when a shirt is worn.
- Sleeves should allow the arms to move forward and upward without pulling the whole garment.
- Short sleeves generally end above the elbow, and should not cut tightly into the arm.

## Shirt Length

- A shirt worn tucked in should be long enough to stay tucked when arms are raised.
- A shirt worn untucked typically ends around the hip area, though relaxed and streetwear styles vary.
- Kurta length ranges from hip length to knee length or longer, and changes the look substantially.
- Very long shirts can shorten the appearance of legs, and very short ones can ride up.

## Trouser Waist

- Trouser waistbands should sit comfortably without digging in or requiring a belt to stay up.
- Sitting comfort matters as much as standing comfort.
- Gaping at the back of the waistband can suggest the waist is too large.
- Rise (the distance from crotch to waistband) affects comfort and appearance; low, mid and high rise suit different bodies and preferences.

## Trouser Length

- A full-length trouser can end at the top of the shoe with a slight or no break, depending on style.
- Cropped trousers end above the ankle, and can look intentional when consistent in cut.
- Wide-leg trousers usually look best when long enough to nearly reach the floor with the shoes worn.
- Hems can be tailored, so length is one of the easier fit issues to correct.

## Dress Fit

- A dress should fit at the shoulders, bust and waist first, since these are harder to alter than length.
- Hem length should suit the occasion and the wearer's comfort.
- Structured dresses need more precise fit than flowing or wrap dresses.
- Movement matters. Check walking, sitting and reaching.

## Jacket / Blazer Fit

- Shoulders should lie flat without a dent or overhang.
- The jacket should close comfortably without pulling into an X shape at the button.
- Sleeve length and jacket length can usually be tailored, but shoulders cannot.
- Room for a shirt or light sweater underneath is worth checking for layering.

## T-Shirt Fit

- Regular T-shirts sit comfortably on the shoulders and chest with sleeve openings that are neither tight nor floppy.
- Slim fits follow the torso more closely.
- Oversized T-shirts have dropped shoulders and extra body length.
- Fabric weight influences drape: lighter jersey clings more, and heavier cotton holds its shape.

## Oversized Fit

- Works well with fitted or tapered pieces on the other half of the outfit to keep proportion clear.
- Suits casual, streetwear and some fashion-forward contexts.
- Can overwhelm very short frames or lightweight fabrics if not balanced, though many people wear it comfortably.

## Slim Fit

- Suits people who like a closer line and stretch-friendly fabrics.
- May feel restrictive in non-stretch fabrics or in hot weather.
- Check that shoulders, arms and thighs move freely.

## Regular Fit

- A dependable choice for everyday and office wear.
- Suits a wide range of body types and occasions.

## Relaxed Fit

- Offers airflow and comfort in warm climates.
- Fabric with some structure holds the shape; very soft fabrics can look shapeless.

## Signs a Garment May Be Too Tight

- Pulling or wrinkling across the chest, waist or hips.
- Buttons straining or gaping.
- Seams showing stress or puckering.
- Restricted arm movement or difficulty sitting.
- Marks or discomfort after wearing.

## Signs a Garment May Be Too Loose

- Fabric hanging or bunching at the shoulders, waist or underarms.
- Sleeves that cover the hands or hang past the intended length.
- Waistbands that slip without a belt.
- Excess fabric that folds or piles around the ankles.
- A shape that feels unintended rather than deliberately relaxed.

## Height and Proportions

- Shorter frames can find that garment length, rise and hem affect how balanced an outfit feels.
- Taller frames can find that sleeve and trouser length, and body length, need checking.
- Longer torsos or longer legs affect where waistlines and hems sit best.
- Many brands offer petite, regular and tall lengths, and tailoring can adjust most of these.
- Proportion is a personal preference. Ask what the user wants rather than assuming.

## Comfort, Fabric and Movement

- Stretch, drape and weight change how a fit feels over the day.
- Natural fibers such as cotton and linen breathe well but crease; knits and blends offer stretch.
- Structured fabrics hold shape but allow less movement, while soft fabrics move easily but show every line.
- Layering needs extra ease. A garment that fits alone may feel tight over another layer.
- Occasions with long sitting, dancing or travel benefit from more movement room.

## Occasion-Appropriate Fit

- Formal and business settings usually favor cleaner, more tailored lines.
- Casual and travel settings allow relaxed or oversized cuts.
- Traditional wear such as sherwanis, lehengas and sarees follows its own fit logic, and usually benefits from professional tailoring or fittings.
- Wedding outfits should allow sitting, walking and photographs comfortably.

## Styling Guidelines for an AI Stylist

- Ask about the user's fit preference before choosing slim, regular, relaxed or oversized.
- Explain the effect of each fit rather than prescribing one.
- Encourage checking size charts and trying garments where returns are possible.
- Pair oversized items with more fitted pieces when the user wants balanced proportions.
- Recommend tailoring for formal and traditional garments.
- Treat comfort as a primary criterion alongside appearance.
