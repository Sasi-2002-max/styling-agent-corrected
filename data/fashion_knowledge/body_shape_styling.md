# Body Shape and Proportion Styling

Body shape is a styling consideration only. It describes proportion, not attractiveness, health or worth. Every body shape can wear every style. The suggestions below describe ways to create visual balance or emphasis when a person wants that effect, and they should be treated as options to experiment with.

## How to Use This Guide

- Use it when a user shares a body shape or asks for proportion-aware suggestions.
- Treat categories as broad approximations. Many people sit between categories or do not fit any of them neatly.
- Personal comfort, style goals and cultural preferences take priority over any silhouette suggestion.
- Focus on what the user wants to highlight or balance, not on something to "fix."
- The app's profile values are `rectangle`, `pear`, `apple`, `hourglass` and `inverted_triangle`. Oval / Round below corresponds to `apple`, and Triangle / Pear corresponds to `pear`.

## Proportion Basics

- Silhouette is shaped by the relationship between shoulders, waist and hips.
- Height, torso length and leg length also affect how an outfit reads.
- Visual balance can be created by drawing attention to different areas with color, pattern, volume and structure.
- Vertical lines can create a lengthened impression; horizontal lines can create width.
- Fabric drape and structure change the effect of a cut as much as the cut itself.

## Rectangle

### General Proportion Characteristics
- Shoulders, waist and hips are similar in width, giving a straighter vertical line.

### Silhouettes That Can Create Visual Balance
- Shapes that add definition at the waist, such as belted styles, wrap styles and peplum details.
- Layers and volume that add dimension at the shoulders or hips.

### Tops
- Ruffles, structured shoulders, wrap tops, cropped or tucked styles that create a waist line.

### Bottoms
- Flared, pleated, paneled or full-volume skirts and trousers can add contrast to a straight line.
- Straight or tailored cuts also work for a clean, streamlined look.

### Dresses
- Belted shirt dresses, fit-and-flare cuts, wrap dresses, A-line styles.

### Jackets and Layers
- Cinched blazers, cropped jackets, belted coats, layered vests.

### Patterns
- Horizontal stripes, color-blocking and mixed textures can add dimension.

### Color Placement
- Contrasting colors at the waist or a lighter top with a deeper bottom can shape the eye's path.

### Things to Experiment With
- Waist belts, structured shoulders, textured fabrics, layered separates.
- In Indian wear: kurtas with an empire or cinched waist, sarees with a belt, lehengas with a defined waistband.

## Triangle / Pear

### General Proportion Characteristics
- Hips are wider than shoulders, with the waist often defined.

### Silhouettes That Can Create Visual Balance
- Details that add interest at the shoulders and neckline, with smooth structure through the hips.

### Tops
- Boat necks, off-shoulder styles, puffed sleeves, embellished or bright tops, structured shoulders.

### Bottoms
- A-line skirts, straight or bootcut trousers, wide-leg trousers, darker or plain bottoms if a subtle look is wanted.

### Dresses
- Fit-and-flare, A-line, empire waist, wrap dresses.

### Jackets and Layers
- Structured blazers that end at the hip line, cropped jackets, shoulder-emphasizing layers.

### Patterns
- Prints, stripes or embellishment near the neckline and shoulders.

### Color Placement
- Lighter or brighter tones on top with deeper or quieter tones below, if the user wants to shift attention upward.

### Things to Experiment With
- Statement necklaces, earrings and neckline details.
- In Indian wear: anarkalis with waist definition, embellished dupattas, kurtas with detailed yokes, flared lehengas.

## Inverted Triangle

### General Proportion Characteristics
- Shoulders or bust are broader than the hips.

### Silhouettes That Can Create Visual Balance
- Volume or detail below the waist with simpler lines above.

### Tops
- V-necks, scoop necks, raglan sleeves, softer knits, simple crew necks.

### Bottoms
- Full skirts, wide-leg trousers, cargo pants, printed or textured bottoms, pleats.

### Dresses
- A-line, fit-and-flare, flared skirts with simpler bodices.

### Jackets and Layers
- Softer, less padded shoulders, longer open layers, drape-front cardigans.

### Patterns
- Prints, stripes or embellishment on the lower half.

### Color Placement
- Deeper or quieter tones on top with brighter or patterned pieces below.

### Things to Experiment With
- Wide-leg or palazzo trousers, statement skirts, flowing fabrics.
- In Indian wear: flared lehengas or skirts, palazzo sets, kurtas with simpler necklines and more detail at the hem.

## Hourglass

### General Proportion Characteristics
- Shoulders and hips are similar in width with a clearly defined waist.

### Silhouettes That Can Create Visual Balance
- Styles that follow or highlight the natural waist while keeping shoulders and hips proportionate.

### Tops
- Wrap tops, fitted tops, V-necks, tucked-in shirts.

### Bottoms
- High-waisted trousers or skirts, pencil skirts, fitted or straight cuts, flared styles that start at the waist.

### Dresses
- Wrap dresses, belted dresses, bodycon or fitted styles, fit-and-flare.

### Jackets and Layers
- Fitted blazers, cropped jackets, belted coats.

### Patterns
- Balanced prints; very oversized or boxy shapes may hide the waist if that is not the intended effect.

### Color Placement
- Coordinated color across the top and bottom keeps the silhouette continuous, while contrast at the waist can add definition.

### Things to Experiment With
- Waist belts, tailored fits, structured pieces.
- In Indian wear: sarees draped to show the waist, fitted blouses, anarkalis with a defined waist, lehengas with a fitted bodice.

## Oval / Round

### General Proportion Characteristics
- The midsection is often the fullest part of the silhouette, with shoulders and hips narrower than the middle.
- Many people in this category have slim legs and arms, which can be highlighted if desired.

### Silhouettes That Can Create Visual Balance
- Long, vertical lines and structured drape that skims the body without clinging.

### Tops
- V-necks, open collars, longer tunics, straight-cut kurtas, structured shirts with slightly longer hems.

### Bottoms
- Straight or slightly tapered trousers, mid-rise or high-rise with a comfortable waistband, flat-front styles.

### Dresses
- Empire waist, A-line, wrap dresses, shirt dresses with fluid fabric.

### Jackets and Layers
- Open-front, longer-line jackets, unbuttoned overshirts, structured blazers with a clean vertical line.

### Patterns
- Vertical stripes, small to medium prints, tonal patterns.

### Color Placement
- Deeper monochrome or tonal outfits create a long vertical line; brighter tones near the face or on legs draw attention there.

### Things to Experiment With
- Layered vertical elements, defined shoulders, printed footwear or accessories.
- In Indian wear: straight or A-line kurtas, long jackets over kurtas, sarees in fluid fabrics, palazzo or straight trousers.

## Styling Guidelines for an AI Stylist

- Ask what the user would like to emphasize or feel comfortable in before applying shape guidance.
- Use neutral wording such as "can create balance" or "can add definition."
- Offer two or three silhouette options and let the user choose.
- Combine body shape guidance with height, comfort, occasion and personal style.
- Avoid the words "flaws," "hide," "disguise" or "problem areas."
- Never rank body shapes or imply that any shape is better suited to fashion than another.
